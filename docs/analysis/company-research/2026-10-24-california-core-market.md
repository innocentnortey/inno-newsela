# California "Core" ELA/ELD Market and State Approval Process

Researched 2026-10-24 for the Newsela hiring-partner project. Primary sources come first: cde.ca.gov pages and documents, State Board of Education (SBE) and Instructional Quality Commission (IQC) agendas, and the Education Code on leginfo. Each claim is tagged **[Fact]** (read in a cited source) or **[Inference]** (my reasoning, not stated by a source). CDE's site sits behind a Radware bot check, so pages were read through a normal-fingerprint browser (nodriver). Nothing was bypassed beyond that.

## Summary (6 lines)

1. **The cycle is live now.** California is running a **2026 ELA/ELD Follow-up Adoption for TK–8**. Programs were due 2026-05-15. Panels deliberated in July, and the IQC voted 2026-09-24. **SBE final action is scheduled for 2026-11-12/13**, a few weeks from today, and Ed Code §60205 (added by AB 1454) requires it by 2027-01-31.
2. **Newsela is already in it.** It submitted **"Threads & Themes" for grades 6–8** in two versions: Program Type 1 (ELA) and Program Type 2 (ELA & ELD). **Both received a panel recommendation for adoption.** The ELA-only version has three small edits and corrections attached. "ca-2026-review" most likely names that submission's review site (inference).
3. **"Core" has a concrete legal meaning.** Each grade needs **180 days** of daily ELA plus **designated ELD**. The program must cover every grade-level CA CCSS ELA standard and every CA ELD standard at all three proficiency levels. It needs a teacher's edition with a pacing guide, embedded formative and summative assessment, and universal-access supports.
4. **Reviewers grade the program through evidence the publisher writes.** That evidence is an **Evaluation Criteria Map** (every criterion, with citations into the program) and **Standards Maps** (every standard, per grade, with citations), plus social-content and accuracy checks. Findings cite lessons by address, for example "Grade 7, Unit 3, Lesson 25, Learning in Action Part B".
5. **The market is large and long-lived.** It is 5.73M students, 1.30M of them in grades 6–8, across 1,011 districts and 1,263 charters. StudySync reaches about 51% of students. Adopted lists last until the next framework, about 8+ years. Historic build costs run to tens of millions of dollars per K–8 program.
6. **[Inference] The tool's job is the evidence.** An AI authoring tool succeeds only if it generates the evidence along with the lessons: per-lesson standards tags that roll up into the state's maps, a 180-day coverage matrix, paired integrated and designated ELD, a social-content log, accessibility conformance, and a human-review trail. It must also respect the rule that **no content may change after delivery** except through edits and corrections.

## 1. What "core" means and why it is a different product

### What it is in practice

- **[Fact]** California law and regulations distinguish *basic* instructional materials from other materials. Basic materials are the full program a teacher uses every day; supplemental materials sit beside them. The SBE adopts "basic instructional materials" for K–8 (Ed Code §60200; AB 1454 digest). Supplemental, add-on components "may not be portrayed as part of the SBE-adopted program". They can still pass a separate **Social Content Review**, but they cannot be marketed as adopted (ITS, "New Edition Substitutions"; 5 CCR §9820[g]).
- **[Fact]** A full Program Type 1 or 2 submission must meet several requirements:
  - **Coverage.** It must "demonstrate alignment with **all** CA CCSS for ELA" (criterion 1.1). Type 2 must also cover **all** CA ELD Standards on the standards maps (1.2). For Type 2's ELD there are "no subset options", and its ELD scope and sequence must span the **Emerging, Expanding and Bridging** levels across Parts I–III of the ELD standards.
  - **Volume.** It must provide "sufficient instructional content for **180 days of instruction**", covering daily lessons and units of ELA time plus **designated ELD** for Types 2, 3 and 5. A planning and pacing guide for 180 days is required (criteria 2.1 and 5.1).
  - **A teacher's edition** that gives "guidance for both daily integrated and designated English language development instruction". It also needs checks for understanding, lesson adjustments, and annotations for English learners (ELs), students with disabilities, advanced learners and students below grade level. Its lesson lists must "cross-reference the standards covered and provide an estimated instructional time for each lesson, chapter, and unit" (Category 5 criteria).
  - **Assessment.** Multiple formative and summative methods are required. Pacing guides must "allot time for ongoing formative assessment". The Observation Protocol for Teachers of English Learners (OPTEL) is named (Category 3 guidance).
  - **Foundational skills, MTSS and dyslexia.** The 2025 guidance adds MTSS-tiered differentiation (MTSS is a multi-tiered system of supports) and supports aligned to the **California Dyslexia Guidelines**, such as text-to-speech with synchronized highlighting.
  - **Media literacy**, including rubrics for judging source credibility.
- **[Fact]** Integrated ELD is language support built into every ELA lesson. Designated ELD is a protected daily block taught to ELD standards, and it "should directly connect to and support standards and content addressed in ELA instruction" (criterion 2.x, 2025 guidance). AB 1454 writes "integrated and designated English language development instruction" into statute (Ed Code §60205[b][1]).

### How Newsela's submission is actually shaped (from the state's own review)

- **[Fact]** Panels described Threads & Themes ELA and ELD as "parallel curricula". The digital program has teacher and student editions for both ELA and ELD. Supporting resources include an ELA Design Framework, a Designated ELD Design Framework, a scope and sequence for each grade, an Assessment Playbook, a Differentiation Playbook and a Universal Access Handbook.
- **[Fact]** Lesson parts named in the findings are *Literacy Lab* (explicit designated ELD and foundational skills), *Learning in Action* (with integrated-ELD instructions), *Huddles* (small groups by MTSS tier) and *Flex Days*. The program provides "a variety of texts and lessons across **180 days** of instruction".
- **[Fact]** The citations reach **Lesson 45** of a unit ("Grade 7, Unit 1, Lesson 45: Look Back").
- **[Inference]** Four units of about 45 lessons is 180 days. The brief's "4 sequential units of ~40 lessons" therefore means roughly 160 taught lessons plus Flex and assessment days. The brief's "40 weeks" overstates the year: California's 180-day year is 36 weeks.

### Why core is a different product from supplemental

**[Inference]** Supplemental content is judged one piece at a time: is this article good and on-level? A core program is judged as a **whole year**: does every standard get taught, practised and assessed somewhere in the 180 days, in a sensible order, with an ELD lesson paired to it and a teacher script for it? Newsela's existing strength, a large leveled-text library, covers only part of criterion 1.7–1.9 (text quality and complexity). Everything else is new authoring: the sequence, the teacher's edition, assessments, designated ELD and pacing.

## 2. California's adoption process and calendar

### Who adopts what

- **[Fact] K–8 is adopted by the state.** The SBE adopts basic materials for K–8 (Ed Code §§60200–60206; 5 CCR §§9500–9530). The 2026 follow-up adds TK.
- **[Fact] 9–12 is adopted locally.** Ed Code §60400 lets each high school district's board choose its own materials. There is no state list.
- **[Fact] The state list is advisory, not mandatory.** "School districts are not mandated to purchase or use SBE-adopted instructional materials and may conduct their own local reviews" (ITS, citing Ed Code §60210).

### The current cycle: the 2026 ELA/ELD Follow-up Adoption (TK–8)

- **[Fact]** The last full ELA/ELD adoption was 2015, adopted 2015-11-04 against the 2014 Framework. The 2026 programs "will be added to the 2015 list", and both lists stay purchasable until a new framework-based adoption (Ed Code §60200[h]; ITS).
- **[Fact]** The legal basis has three parts:
  - The follow-up adoption runs under Ed Code §60227.
  - **AB 121** (2025 budget trailer, Section 89) funded guidance written with WestEd. The SBE adopted that *Guidance to Support the Follow-up Adoption* on 2025-11-06.
  - **AB 1454** (Rivas, Ch. 471, Stats. 2025, signed 2025-10-09) added **Ed Code §60205**. The SBE must adopt K–8 ELA/ELD materials **on or before 2027-01-31**. The materials must align to the Framework (integrated and designated ELD), to "evidence-based means of teaching foundational reading skills… explicit and systematic instruction", and to the state dyslexia guidelines. They may focus on specific skill subsets.

### Who reviews: IMRs, CREs and facilitators

**[Fact]** This is the process the brief calls the "Instructional Materials Advisory Panel / Content Review Expert process":

- **Instructional Materials Reviewers (IMRs).** Mostly credentialed TK–12 teachers. At least one has experience teaching ELs and at least one has experience teaching students with disabilities.
- **Content Review Experts (CREs).** For ELA, a master's degree or higher plus 5+ years of experience, or a doctorate.
- **Facilitators.** Usually IQC members; they drive the panel to consensus.

**[Fact]** How the review runs:

- Reviewers review independently first, then deliberate as a panel.
- On day 1 of deliberations, panels send written questions to the publisher. The publisher answers in a formal presentation on day 2.
- Reports state "met / not met" per category, grade by grade, cite example locations in the program, and list required edits and corrections.
- Publishers may not contact reviewers from January to November 2026, or Commissioners from May 15 to November 2026, outside public meetings (5 CCR §9514).
- Reports of Findings may not be used in marketing (5 CCR §9517[n]).

### Participants (Program Types 1 and 2, grades 6–8 and K–8)

**[Fact]**
- Type 1, ELA: American Reading Company; Amplify (CKLA and ELA); Fishtank; Imagine Learning (StudySync California); McGraw Hill (Emerge! K–5, Summit! 6–8); **Newsela (Threads & Themes, ELA, 6–8)**; Open Up Resources (EL Education); Savvas (myView, myPerspectives); ThinkCERCA.
- Type 2, ELA/ELD: Amplify (with Language Studio); Benchmark (Max Literacy, K–5); Collaborative Classroom; HMH (Into Reading K–5, Into Literature 6–8); Imagine Learning; Inquiry by Design; Kiddom (EL Education K–8); McGraw Hill; **Newsela (Threads & Themes, ELA & ELD, 6–8)**; Open Up; Reading Horizons; Savvas (with myVoice ELD); ThinkCERCA.
- The round also includes biliteracy, intervention, specialized ELD, TK and partial-program (single strand) submissions.

### What "ca-2026-review" might be

**[Inference]** "ca-2026-review" is probably not a state system. Two state requirements create a need for a publisher-hosted review site:

1. Reviewers, IQC, SBE members and the ~15 Learning Resources Display Centers (LRDCs) need **login access to the digital program**. The CDE participants page publishes public review credentials for several publishers. Display centers need access for at least 2 years after adoption.
2. 5 CCR §9523(b) requires **all student materials to be posted at a URL for free public review until SBE action**. Sign-in and watermarks are allowed; restricted access is not.

So "ca-2026-review" most likely names Newsela's reviewer or public-review instance of Threads & Themes for this adoption. That makes it the product surface the state's reviewers cited ("ELA Courses › View Course as a Teacher › Grade 7 › Unit 3 › Lesson 25"). Confirm with Newsela.

### Evaluation criteria: the five categories

**[Fact]** These come from Framework Chapter 12, updated by the 2025 Guidance. Program Type 2's criteria map runs 134 pages.

1. **ELA/ELD Content and Alignment with Standards.** All standards. The Framework's five themes: meaning making, language development, effective expression, content knowledge, foundational skills. Current research. Grammar and spelling (Ed Code §60045). Text quality, range and complexity. Read-alouds. Independent reading. Foundational skills and fluency. Decodables for K–2. Category 1 is the gate: a program must meet **all** of it to be recommended.
2. **Program Organization.** 180 days. Scope and sequence for ELA and for ELD Parts I–III at every proficiency level. Explicit designated-ELD guidance. Embedded formative assessment. Media literacy.
3. **Assessment.** Multiple measures. Student self-assessment. Data use for differentiation. EL progress tools such as OPTEL, linked to ELPAC (the state English proficiency test) and dyslexia screeners.
4. **Universal Access.** The panels' reports title it "Access and Equity". Lessons and teacher's editions aligned with ELD standards for all ELs, including newcomers at the Emerging level. Students with disabilities, dyslexia, advanced learners. MTSS tiers. Universal Design for Learning (UDL).
5. **Instructional Planning and Teacher Support.** 180-day pacing guide. Daily lesson adjustment. Daily integrated and designated ELD guidance. Explicit objectives. Standards cross-referenced, with estimated time per lesson, chapter and unit. Annotations for modelling, thinking aloud and deconstructing language. Cross-linguistic transfer charts.

### Social content review

**[Fact]** Panels also run a social content review (5 CCR §9525, §§9810–9830). Citations are fixed through edits and corrections. The standards are Ed Code §§60040–60045 and §60048, plus the SBE's *Standards for Evaluating Instructional Materials for Social Content* (2013). Categories:

- Male and female roles: roughly equal portrayal, occupations and emotions.
- Ethnic and cultural groups: fair proportion, mainstream U.S. life, not only root culture.
- Sexual orientation and gender identity.
- Older persons; people with disabilities.
- Entrepreneur and labor; religion: no ridicule, no indoctrination.
- Ecology; dangerous substances: never glamorized, hazards noted.
- Thrift, fire safety, humane treatment.
- **Brand names and logos**: not allowed unless educationally necessary or incidental.

### District adoption of non-state-adopted materials

- **[Fact] Ed Code §60210.** A local educational agency (LEA) may use standards-aligned materials not adopted by the SBE. If it does, "a majority of the participants of any review process… are classroom teachers who are assigned to the subject area or grade level".
- **[Fact] New with AB 1454 (Ed Code §60205[c]–[d]).** Once the SBE adopts, it must update the *Guidance for Local Instructional Materials Adoptions*. LEAs **must follow** that guidance and the Ed Code §60002 process. An LEA that picks **non-adopted** materials "**shall certify** that the instructional materials align to the criteria" (Framework, evidence-based foundational skills, dyslexia guidelines). LEAs may combine several materials that together cover all standards.
- **[Fact] Williams sufficiency (Ed Code §60119).** To receive instructional-materials funds, each board holds a public hearing by the end of the **8th week** of school. By resolution, it determines whether every pupil has sufficient standards-aligned textbooks or materials in ELA, "including the English language development component of an adopted program". Any shortfall must be fixed within two months.
- **[Inference]** Together these mean that even outside the state list, a California district needs alignment evidence it can sign. A publisher that can hand over state-format standards and criteria maps makes that certification cheap. That is a sales asset in the 9–12 market and with non-list buyers.

### Recent laws and policies to know

**[Fact]**
- **AB 1454 (2025):** described above. It also sets literacy preparation standards for reading specialists (by 2028-01-01) and administrators (by 2028-09-01).
- **The 2025–26 budget:** a $480M literacy package. About $200M goes to literacy coaches and reading specialists, $200M to TK–5 professional development, and $40M to K–2 screening. A $1.7B discretionary block grant can be spent on anything. The earlier $3.6B Arts, Music and Instructional Materials Block Grant can be encumbered through 2025–26.
- **Federal ADA Title II web rule:** WCAG 2.1 AA for public entities' web content, which covers districts. Compliance dates were extended by an interim final rule to **2027-04-26** (entities with population of 50k or more) and **2028-04-26** (smaller and special districts).

## 3. What state and district reviewers actually check, and what the publisher hands in

### Required publisher evidence (state adoption)

**[Fact; ITS and 5 CCR §9517]**

| Deliverable | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Evaluation Criteria Map** (Word, not PDF, plus hard copy) | One row per criterion. The publisher fills a "Publisher Citations" column showing where in the program each criterion is met. Reviewers mark Met Yes/No and add notes. |
| **Standards Maps**, per grade (Word plus hard copy) | One row per standard, e.g. `RL.6.1`, with citations. ELD maps go row by row **per proficiency level**, e.g. `PI.6.1.Em`, `PI.6.1.Ex` (Em, Ex, Br = Emerging, Expanding, Bridging). |
| Program Description | At most 6 pages; tells reviewers how to navigate the program. |
| Submission Form (Excel) | Every component with an ISBN, DOI or other ID of 13 characters or fewer. Each print component needs a digital equivalent. Components must be unbundled. |
| Complete program | In the same form it will be sold. Digital access for reviewers and display centers. Inventory list. |
| Public student-materials URL | Free public access until the SBE vote (5 CCR §9523[b]). |
| Presentations | About 1 hour at training. A formal answer to panel questions on day 2 of deliberations. |
| Edits and corrections package | Before/after pages within 60 days of the SBE action. Final copies within 60 days of CDE sign-off. |
| Accessible files | Within 30 days of request: unlocked PDF of all print student materials (≤500 MB per file), cover JPGs, 5 print copies. NIMAS files to NIMAC are recommended. |

### Rules that bind content

**[Fact]**
- **Freeze.** No change after 2026-05-15 except edits and corrections. "**Rewrites**" are disallowed: revising a section, chapter or page, adding new content, or moving material between grades (5 CCR §9510, §9517[k]). After adoption, changes go through a fee-based revision process about every 2 years (5 CCR §9526).
- **Standards.** Cite only SBE-approved standards. No national or other-state standards (Ed Code §60005[b]; 5 CCR §9517[h]).
- **Accuracy.** "All aspects… must be accurate", content and terminology alike (Framework Ch. 12). Grammar and spelling are a criterion.
- **Credits.** Only people "who actually contributed" may be credited, with the role they served (5 CCR §9517[i]).
- **Privacy and design.** Student privacy under Ed Code §§49073–49079.7 and SOPIPA (Bus. & Prof. Code §§22584–22585). Universal design for print and digital (Ed Code §60061.8). Lowest-price and most-favoured-nation pricing (Ed Code §§60060–60063.5).

### What reviewers look for in practice

**[Fact; read from Newsela's two Reports of Findings]**
- Citations point to a **navigable location**: course › teacher view › grade › unit › lesson › lesson part. Examples: "G6.U3.L18: Learning in Action, Part B" (RI.6.8, RI.6.9) and "G8.U4.L8: The Last Cuentista, Socratic Seminar 1" (L.8.1c, L.8.3a, RL.8.1…).
- Reviewers check that **terminology is used consistently** with the CA CCSS and ELD standards across all teacher guidance (criterion 5.9). They also check that ELD lessons connect to the paired ELA lesson.
- Edits and corrections show reviewers read at the **word level**, for example syllabification and schwa pronunciation in decoding examples.

### District-level review

- **[Fact]** Districts run teacher-majority committees (Ed Code §60210[c]) and Williams hearings. After AB 1454, districts using non-adopted programs must certify alignment.
- **[Fact] EdReports** is not required by California. Many districts use its three gateways: (1) text quality and standards alignment; (2) building knowledge; (3) usability, assessment and differentiation. A program must clear gateway 1 before the later gateways are reviewed. California districts reach EdReports through the CalCurriculum partnership.
- **[Inference]** Large districts often pilot two or three programs. They will also ask for evidence of efficacy; Newsela has an ESSA Level II study of a *different* product, Newsela ELA.

## 5. Implications for an AI lesson-generation and review tool *(all inference)*

**The main idea.** California does not review "good lessons". It reviews a **frozen year of instruction plus the publisher's own evidence map**, then checks a sample of that evidence. So the tool's real output is a lesson corpus that can **prove its own coverage** at the granularity reviewers cite. The prose is only part of it.

**Where Newsela is today.** Its first-wave 6–8 program is already submitted, frozen and recommended. So the tool's value lies in the next jobs:

- the edits and corrections cycle (word-level fixes, before/after evidence);
- the biennial revision windows;
- expansion to K–5;
- local-adoption buyers who must certify alignment;
- 9–12 and other states.

**What the tool must produce, and why:**

1. **A stable address for every lesson part**, e.g. `G7.U3.L25.LearningInAction.PartB`. Reviewers cite in this form, and the Standards and Criteria Maps must point to it.
2. **Per-lesson standards tags with a stated role**: introduced, practised or assessed. Only SBE standards are allowed, with no national or other-state codes. ELD tags go per proficiency level (`PI.6.1.Em/Ex/Br`).
3. **Generated Standards Maps and an Evaluation Criteria Map in the CDE Word templates.** Rows are pre-filled with citations from the tags. Every row must have at least one citation, otherwise there is a Category 1 risk.
4. **A 180-day coverage matrix and an arc check** across units 1–4. Every grade-level standard must appear and must be assessed. Scaffolds should fade, text complexity should rise toward RL/RI.10, and nothing should be duplicated or orphaned across units. Flex and assessment days must be accounted for in the pacing guide.
5. **Integrated and designated ELD paired with every ELA lesson.** It must use the same essential question and anchor text, and be checked for "directly connect[s] to" the ELA content.
6. **Teacher-edition completeness checks per lesson**: objectives (learning, language, instructional), estimated time, checks for understanding, adjust-up and adjust-down notes, EL/SWD/advanced annotations, and MTSS tier routes.
7. **Assessment records**: item-to-standard alignment, rubrics, answer keys, and formative checkpoints placed in the pacing.
8. **A social-content log** per text and image, covering the Ed Code §§60040–60048 categories, brand names, substances, and the representation balance across the year. Judging "fair proportion" needs year-level counts, not per-lesson checks.
9. **Accuracy and terminology linting**: consistent CA CCSS and ELD terms (criterion 5.9), grammar and spelling (Ed Code §60045), and decoding and syllabification checks (Newsela's actual citations were here).
10. **Accessibility conformance**: WCAG 2.1 AA for the digital program, UDL features, text-to-speech readiness, and an unlocked PDF and NIMAS export path for the CDE's Clearinghouse for Specialized Media and Technology (CSMT).
11. **A human-review evidence trail per lesson**: who reviewed what, when, against which criterion, and what changed. This supports the "only people who actually contributed" credit rule, district certification, and any reviewer questions about AI authorship. Neither the ITS nor the 2025 Guidance mentions AI (searched), so how AI authorship would be treated is an open question to raise with Newsela.
12. **Change control that matches the freeze.** Versioned snapshots at delivery. Diffs classified as an *edit and correction* (allowed) or a *rewrite* (not allowed). Before/after page exports for the 60-day corrections package.

**Open questions for Newsela**

- Is "ca-2026-review" the reviewer or public-review instance of Threads & Themes?
- Is the next target K–5, a revision, or another state?
- How much of the current 6–8 program was human-authored, and where did the time go?
