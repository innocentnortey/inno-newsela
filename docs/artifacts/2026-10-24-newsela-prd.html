1 · The story so far

Newsela is known for one thing teachers love: the same news article at five reading levels. This year it is moving into "core" instruction for California. That means a full year of daily lessons that a district adopts for every teacher to follow.

Its first core program already exists. Threads & Themes covers grades 6–8 in English (ELA) and English Language Development (ELD, teaching for students still learning English). It is 4 units of about 45 lessons per grade: 180 days. The content was frozen on 2026-05-15. State review panels recommended both versions in summer 2026, and the State Board votes on 2026-11-12/13. Fact Profile §6; audit §1.

It was made by freelancers and AI drafts, with staff checking the drafts. A 2026 job post says the content designer will "own the review of contributor- and AI-generated lessons". Fact Audit §1 [Site §5.1].

So why write this brief? The next programs need the same production line, faster. Grades 9–12 are "coming 2027". A 60-day package of corrections follows the vote. Other states may follow. Each new program needs about 1,000 lessons, every one proven to the state's bar. Fact for the dates · Proposal for "about 1,000" (an assumption from [CA §4]). Framing §1.

Our user is the content reviewer: a Learning Experience Designer (LXD), one of the brief's "20+ reviewers".

Layer 1: the why-chain

One lesson, before and after Fact Proposal

Take Lesson 4 (sheet row 8), The Lightning Thief. The sheet lists its standards as RL.6.1, 6.2 L.6.4. "6.2" is not a real code.

Today (fact)

A reviewer gets a prose draft. They spot that "6.2" is broken. They look up what each standard says. They hunt for the activity that teaches it. They check every quote against the book. Then they retype the lesson into Newsela's system.

In our tool (proposal)

  1. The validation report flags 6.2 before any money is spent.
  2. The draft arrives in Newsela's own lesson shape: Launch 5 → Literacy Lab 10 → Learning in Action 30 → Look Back 5 minutes.
  3. Each standard links to the part that teaches it and the question that checks it.
  4. A quote with no source becomes a "Reviewer to insert" box, which blocks Approve.

Framing §2 "Concrete example"; PRD §13 (WP-1 is row 8).

Branches we rejected Your decision

  • Generate faster. The brief allows "a few minutes per lesson"; generation is not the slow step.
  • Auto-approve when checks pass. The state credits only people "who actually contributed" (5 CCR §9517(i)).
  • A Google-Docs-grade editor. Nothing asks for it; Yjs (live co-editing) stays minimal (AD-6).
  • Fine-tune on their lessons. Newsela's Terms §9(b) forbid it.

5 · The traps we found

A trap is a line in the brief that, read literally, leads to a bad build. These are 8 of the 26, the most severe first. Each shows the brief's words, the trap, and what we do instead. All 26 are in the audit §5.

T-1 · Invented quotes Your decision

Critical · legal, ethical, engineering

The brief's words
"referencing text/novel indicated in the provided unit outline sheet"
The trap
No source texts are supplied, so the model fills the gap from memory: invented quotes, or passages of The Lightning Thief (in copyright). Statewide, one error becomes hundreds.
Our response
Reference, don't reproduce. Quotes only from supplied text; otherwise a "Reviewer to insert" box. A rights status on every excerpt.

AD-8, GR-1–GR-3 (decided 2026-10-24). How we check it is PD-5, a Proposal for §3.

T-2 · "Aligned" with no check Proposal

Critical · engineering, ethical, legal

The brief's words
"what's important is that we generate standard aligned content"
The trap
"Aligned" is the whole value, and nobody defines it. Copying the codes into the header feeds false claims into a state purchase.
Our response
Check in layers: codes resolve to the official list; each standard maps to a part that teaches it and a question that checks it; a person confirms. Never a bare "aligned" badge.

PD-6, settled at §3. The no-badge rule (GR-4) is Your decision.

T-3 · A rubber-stamp Approve Your decision

Critical · ethical, engineering

The brief's words
"once reviewed/updated it is flagged as complete"
The trap
Approve becomes "approve all 40". AI rewrites slip in after review.
Our response
One lesson at a time, no bulk approve. Approve is off while blocks stand. Approval is pinned to the version seen. An append-only log.

AD-11, PD-10 (decided 2026-10-24). The pinning mechanics (PD-7) wait for §3.

T-4 · The example site is private Your decision

Critical (legal)

The brief's words
"feel free to crawl as many examples as needed via that link"
The trap
The site is very likely the state-review copy of a program not yet adopted. Showing it in public would disclose it.
Our response
Crawled pages stay in a git-ignored folder. Unit 4 is held out as the test set. The repo is private. The video shows only our output.

AD-3, AD-4, GR-9 (decided 2026-10-24). Whether the site is confidential is AQ-1 Unknown.

T-5 · The outline is dirty Your decision

High · engineering, legal

The brief's words
"That outline is a google spreadsheet of ~40 lessons"
The trap
Cell A1 says U4; every filename says U3. 41 lessons + 6 flex days = 47 days. RL.6.9.a does not exist. A simple parser drops 6.2, and every later check passes against the wrong list.
Our response
A strict cleaner plus a validation report a person confirms before Generate turns on. Each defect becomes a test.

AD-9 (decided 2026-10-24); R-3–R-5.

T-6 · Six products in 1–2 days Your decision

Critical (scope)

The brief's words
Six requirements, a bonus, ProseMirror + Yjs, "1-2 days"
The trap
Build a thin layer of everything, so nothing holds up.
Our response
The smallest slice we can defend, with a named cut order (section 7).

AD-1, PD-17 (decided 2026-10-24).

T-7 · An HTML blob in the editor Your decision

Critical · engineering

The brief's words
"ProseMirror and Yjs for the frontend/collaborative editor"
The trap
Load generated HTML into the editor. It looks done, but nothing can check, compare or export it.
Our response
Newsela's own typed lesson record. Standards and minutes are form fields. One logged-in sync server, saved to our database.

AD-5, AD-6 (decided 2026-10-24).

T-9 · "Async is fine" hides a job system Your decision

High · engineering

The brief's words
"a few minutes per lesson is ok"; "Vercel or Railway … feel free"
The trap
47 rows × ~3 minutes is ~2 hours one after another. Serverless functions time out, so jobs die mid-lesson.
Our response
A database-backed job queue in a long-running worker: each lesson its own job, safe to retry and resume, with a spend cap.

AD-14 (decided 2026-10-24). The hosting spend is not decided: section 9.

6 · The metrics chain

How we will know the tool works, and how that reaches money. Labels: [T] a target we set, [A] an assumption we would check, [M] a measurement. No metric has a baseline: Newsela has not told us its review time per lesson (AQ-14).

Why this north star Your decision

It is the cost. About 30 minutes of review at about $40 an hour is about $20 a lesson. Model spend is well under $1. So review is about 98% of the cost [A].

It can be measured for every lesson from activity events.

Reading time sets the scale. A published Unit 4 teacher lesson is a median 3,306 words [M]. That is about 13 minutes just to read [calculation].

PD-1; framing §3.2; PRD §4. Rejected: "lessons generated" (vanity) and ARR (years away, can't be credited to a build).

Why it needs guards Your decision

Lower is better, so the cheapest way to "improve" it is to skim. That is why it never appears alone.

G-2 flags review faster than half the read time. G-3 plants errors and counts the catches.

Framing §3.2, §7 (the best objection and our answer); GR-15: shown in aggregate only, never per person.

Where this comes up in the project

Newsela asked for a tool that writes a unit of lessons from an outline, then lets 20+ staff edit and approve them. Before building anything, we read the brief three ways (legal, ethical, engineering), studied the company and the California market, and wrote down who uses the tool and what success means. This page shows the result. Every later stage builds on it: the worked examples (§2), the hard problems (§3) and the PRD interview (§4).

The problem we're solving

We need you to agree on what problem we are solving. In one line: cut the reviewer minutes each lesson needs to pass approval, without lowering the state's bar. For example, Lesson 4 arrives with its broken code flagged, each standard linked to the part that teaches it, and a "Reviewer to insert" box where a quote would have been invented. The reviewer checks evidence instead of rebuilding it.

Why it's hard

The brief asks for a generator, and the obvious build is a generator. But our research says drafting is already partly solved, so a better generator would not help the reviewer much. Choosing review as the target changes the whole build: the north star, the guardrails, which features are core and which are cut. If the framing is wrong, all of that is aimed at the wrong thing, and there are only about 27 hours to build.

Why we're asking you, and what your answer changes

The zero-to-hero sequence needs your sign-off at this gate before Stage 2 starts. Your pre-approval covers our recommended choices, but not the framing as a whole: that is the gate itself. If you approve, we start Stage 2 tonight with three worked examples (rows 8, 9 and 25), keeping the calendar in section 7. If you change the framing (say, aim at generation speed instead), the north star, the core list and the cut order are rewritten first, before Stage 2 starts.
