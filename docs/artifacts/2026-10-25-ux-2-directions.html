The story so far

Newsela's reviewers will spend long days in this tool, reading one 50-minute lesson at a time, fixing it, and approving it. So it has to feel calm, editorial, expert, warm and accountable. Those five words come from the feel brief.

Searching "education dashboard" would have shown what school software looks like today, which is often dated. So we searched for the feel and the screen shapes instead, in tools that already do them well: document review, code review, calm issue trackers, data imports. Four families stood out. Each became a direction with its own look sheet.

Where each one sits

The four were chosen to sit far apart, so the choice is real. Two of them are light and cool (D1, D3), so they separate on softness and shape.

D1 · Clear Ledger Proposal

The lesson is a document on a desk; the tool is a quiet editor's frame around it.

ThemeLight · cool white · ink blue
SoftnessMedium corners, hairlines
DensityAiry
ShapesPage sheet, status stepper, right rail

Good at: Best fit for long, careful reading and for showing evidence beside the text. Feels like a premium document tool.

Watch out: Can look plain if the evidence rail is empty; the stepper takes vertical space.

D2 · Night Desk Proposal

A focused workbench for experts who live in it all day.

ThemeDark first · graphite · signal green
SoftnessHard corners, no shadows
DensityDense
ShapesGrouped rows, tabs, mono IDs

Good at: Fastest to scan; feels like the premium tools engineers love. Approve-a-version reads like code review.

Watch out: Dark reading of ~3,300-word lessons tires some readers; teachers may find it cold.

D3 · Soft Studio Proposal

A friendly team space that makes review feel shared.

ThemeLight · periwinkle wash · indigo
SoftnessVery soft, pills, soft shadows
DensityAiry
ShapesCards, three panes

Good at: Warmest and most welcoming; review feels like teamwork, which speaks to reviewers wary of AI.

Watch out: Least dense: the 47-row tables need more scrolling. Its rounded font shows fully in Safari only.

D4 · Warm Workroom Proposal

A well-run workshop: the numbers and the trail are always visible.

ThemeLight · white canvas · walnut and terracotta
SoftnessFirm corners, ruled tables
DensityDense
ShapesTables, banded headers, approval timeline

Good at: Most accountable: import errors, counts and the approval trail are always in view. Warm without being cream.

Watch out: The walnut sidebar is a strong flavour; dense tables can feel administrative.

Your shortlist Recommend: D1, D4, D2

Where this comes up in the project

Every screen we build next uses one of these looks: the validation report, the unit board, the lesson workspace and the approval screen. The look you choose also shapes how the demo video feels to Newsela.

The problem

Which directions deserve a clickable prototype? Each one you shortlist gets linked screens you can click through. Then you pick one.

Why it's hard

All four fit the feel brief, in different ways. A dark tool is fast to scan but tiring to read in. A soft tool is welcoming but less dense. A look is easier to judge by clicking than by reading.

Why we're asking you

Taste is yours, and it's cheap to keep three alive now: each linked prototype takes under an hour. Picking too early is what cost hours on the earlier project.

Proposal Shortlist D1 Clear Ledger (best fit for long reading with evidence beside it), D4 Warm Workroom (most accountable, warm without cream) and D2 Night Desk (the strongest contrast, to test whether you want dark). My first choice is D1. You can also pick one outright, or ask for a fifth direction.
